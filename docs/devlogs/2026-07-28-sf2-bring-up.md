# SF2 devlog: first playable bring-up

**Date:** 2026-07-28  
**Status:** Early native integration; not yet a playable campaign

Today *Syphon Filter 2* crossed from archive compatibility into recognizable,
controllable gameplay. This is still an engineering scene-test rather than a
finished port, but the disc is now providing the real mission geometry,
textures, actors, animation data, object records, and item definitions.

No proprietary game data was added to the repository. Everything described
below is decoded at runtime from a user-supplied NTSC-U BIN/CUE image.

## What now works

- Both SF2 discs are recognized from their executable profiles.
- All 21 SF2 campaign mission packages construct successfully:
  - Disc 1: 8/8
  - Disc 2: 13/13
- Mission resources, titles, room tables, actors, objects, animations, texture
  banks, and initial player locations are mapped.
- Native high-resolution rendering, PGXP, widescreen presentation, and high
  refresh rates run with SF2 mission data.
- The modern keyboard movement contract and horizontal/vertical mouse-look are
  active in chase and first-person aiming views.
- Gabe and Lian can move, run, strafe, roll, crouch, aim, fire, reload, and
  interact through the shared native controller.
- Native gunfire raycasts against actors and the world, including impact
  decals.
- SF2/SF3's eight-direction movement semantics are translated to WASD.
- Flagged sequel object classes are decoded by their base family. For example,
  `0x5000e` is handled as a door and `0x20034` as an exit-family object.
- Closed sequel doors block movement; interacting opens linked door halves
  together.
- Lockers can swap to their authored open model, and switches can operate their
  linked source.
- SF2 NPC and pickup item numbers are translated through the sequel item table
  instead of being incorrectly cast as SF1 inventory slots.
- Native campaign carry snapshots can now preserve sequel inventory,
  ammunition, health, and armor.

## Mission 2: McKenzie Airbase Interior

AIRBASE was the first useful grounded traversal test. Lian now starts at the
authored source and can leave the opening room, enter the hallway, and roam
through multiple rooms. This was confirmed interactively.

The original failure was not a closed door. SF1's taller collision capsule and
an XZ-only wall-edge test turned below-floor and overhead doorway edges into
full-height barriers. SF2/SF3 now use the narrower sequel capsule, require
vertical overlap, and test a quad's perimeter rather than its internal
diagonal.

AIRBASE also now has the beginning of its mission state:

- Lian starts without a weapon or armor.
- The two-minute adrenaline countdown is displayed.
- The authored adrenaline source stops the countdown and restores health.
- The linked combat-gear chain grants the .45, armor, and hand taser.

At the end of the original 2026-07-28 session, low starting health and the
complete objective/parameter state were still deferred. The continuation below
adds a documented reversible approximation and the first native state.

## Mission 3: Colorado Interstate 70

I-70 renders recognizably and Gabe starts at the correct authored location.
The level now gives him the knife seen in the original opening rather than the
SF1 default pistol loadout.

The knife is an explicit sequel item using SF1's otherwise unused inventory
slot 3. Its recovered executable record supplies:

- `KNIFE.GMD`
- 100 close-range damage
- zero ranged damage
- no ammunition

The model loads successfully and native knife attacks use a short melee trace
without a muzzle flash or wall bullet decal.

I-70 is not wired correctly as an encounter yet. In the original mission,
Chance and the allied soldiers occupy the enemy's attention while Gabe begins
behind a guard, performs the opening knife takedown, and takes his weapon.
Our actors currently enter generic native AI instead of that authored
distraction sequence. Reconstructing this opening encounter is the next
mission-specific gameplay boundary.

The authored supply sources are already decoded as the Glock 17, M16, shotgun,
armor, and fragmentation grenade.

## Data-format findings

Several apparent rendering failures were format deltas rather than corrupt
assets:

- Only EMD flag bit 4 selects `VRAM1.HOG`; the rest of the high nibble contains
  unrelated scene flags.
- SF1/SF2 EMD transformed-vertex offsets use three-byte units; SF3 uses
  two-byte units.
- SF1/SF2 non-flat HMD transformed vertices use twelve-byte units, while SF3
  uses eight.
- Empty sequel GMD/HMD parts may retain the retail inverted-bounds sentinel.
  It is accepted only when the corresponding part has no vertices.
- AIRBASE begins in visibility state 2 while standing on an always-resident
  connector model. Resident geometry must not take ownership of the current
  portal state.

These corrections also allowed all 19 currently mapped SF3 campaign packages
to construct, although SF3 gameplay remains later work.

### Audio and script-runtime blocker

The early inspection correctly established that `AIRBASE.SS` is compiled

narrowly. Later executable analysis proves that `.SS` is the shared compiled

guest runtime. Connecting mission audio safely requires the shared `.SS`
runtime bindings plus sequel XA/SPU support. No placeholder music or guessed
sound mapping was added.

## Whole-game reverse-engineering continuation — 2026-07-29

The continuation deliberately moved beyond Missions 2 and 3. The goal was to
map reusable campaign-wide code and data boundaries across both SF2 discs and
SF3 before adding more mission-specific approximations.

### Whole-executable and call-flow maps

`sf_tool` now emits:

- 2,960 SF2 function seeds from either individual disc and a 2,964-seed
  whole-campaign union of both mission/resident overlay closures;
- 2,917 SF3 seeds;
- unique exact and normalized structural SF2↔SF3 function matches;
- seed provenance (`entry`, `direct_call`, `overlay`, or `prologue_only`);
- exact embedded-asset-range markers for function seeds and call sites.

The asset markers matter. SF2 has 329 seeds inside proven embedded
animation/texture/archive data, 323 of them discovered only as apparent
prologues. The cleaner executable baseline is 2,631 seeds. Function
coalescing also now folds a preceding linear compiler prefix into the
following stack prologue instead of reporting two false functions. The final
entry of each embedded archive remains an upper-bound range and is not
automatically excluded.

The two SF2 discs have byte-identical executables and resident overlays, but
their mission overlays contribute different extra entry points. The union
mapper verifies executable identity before combining those closures.

### Resident overlays and embedded archives

The resident runtime bases are now proven from internal calls, prologues, and
adjacent overlay extents:

- SF2 `MENU`/`MOVIE` at `0x80142150`, `TITLE` at `0x8014b950`, and `INIT`
  at `0x80158878`;
- SF3 `MENU`/`MENU2`/`MOVIE` at `0x80146950`, `TITLE`/`TITLE2` at
  `0x80150950`, and `INIT` at `0x8015e978`.

SF2 contributes 493 resident function seeds; SF3 contributes 691 across its
six variants. There are 257 unique structural resident matches, including 148
`INIT` pairs and 56 `MOVIE` pairs.

Five embedded HOG-like archives were catalogued in each sequel executable.
SF2 has 365 named entries: 166 animations, 87 UI/inventory textures, 97
weapon/effect textures, eight resident-package entries, and seven title
assets. SF3 has 398 entries in the same organization.

### Full mission-object corpus

The raw sequel object tail is preserved as four handler parameters plus one
handler-state halfword. Neutral names are intentional because meanings differ
by class.

The maps cover:

- SF2: 666 definitions and 3,791 placed objects across all 21 missions;
- SF3: 541 mapped definitions and 3,869 placed objects across all 19 missions;
- source index, class, models, room set, transform, attributes, AI parameter,
  path, link, health, and raw handler fields.

SF2 uses 121 class families. SF3 adds family `0x84` (`OBJ_VEH`), proving that
its object-handler table contains 133 entries rather than SF2's 132. The
mapper's previous SF3 bound was corrected.

AIRBASE sources 22/23/24 are paired with 25/26/27 as authored
class-`4c`/`4d` records. Source 24 is therefore not simply an arbitrary “third
volume.” Its native finale bridge remains provisional until the shared script
program is bound.

### `.SS` container and VM

The retail INIT loaders at SF2 `0x80164fdc` and SF3 `0x8016b348` load each
mission's `.SS`, relocate six program pointers, reset program state, locate
the program named `LEVEL`, and activate it.

The parsed retail corpus contains:

| Build | Programs | Event records | Reachable action/control words |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| SF2 Disc 1 | 66 | 792 | 4,292 |
| SF2 Disc 2 | 161 | 2,325 | 11,158 |
| SF3 | 360 | 4,573 | 23,837 |

Confirmed program fields now include format byte, mutable-variable count,
first serialized variable (or `0xff`), timer count, event stream, variable
table, timer table, name pointer, and serialized blob size. Event records
carry an event ID, selector/filter, length, and action stream. Event `0`
decrements timers and dispatches event `8` on expiry.

The native parser now retains the initial variable and signed timer
halfwords, validates both tables against each program's serialized extent,
and passed all 227 SF2 plus 360 SF3 retail programs. This is reusable VM state,
not an AIRBASE/HWAY approximation.

The core SF2 VM is mapped from predicate decode at `0x800b2b08` through
program event dispatch at `0x800b36a0`, active-list iteration, name lookup,
activation/deactivation, save-state serialization, restore, and `LEVEL`
startup. Exact/aligned SF3 counterparts are recorded for every core entry.

The VM has:

- 64 predicate descriptors (55 populated in SF2, 59 in SF3);
- 128 action descriptors, all populated in both games;
- 118 action slots used by the SF2 retail corpus;
- 108 action slots used by SF3;
- fourteen used only by SF2 and four used only by SF3.

The action walker follows both predicate outcomes, deduplicates loops, and
validates every reachable target against its enclosing event record. It does
not execute handler side effects. No guessed native interpreter was added.
